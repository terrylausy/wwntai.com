COOPERATION MODELS

Five Cooperation Models

Flexible and negotiable, from basic labs to deep research commercialization, advancing in stages

Model 0 · Brand School-enterprise brand co-building

Joint university robot brand co-building

Co-build a university-owned robot brand — the university provides branding, training space, faculty, and student teams; the enterprise provides arm hardware, core technology, production lines, and market operations.

University brand and facilities
  • University provides brand qualification and IP ownership
  • Provides professional training lab space
  • Provides faculty and student R&D teams
Enterprise technology empowerment
  • Provides core hardware like the OpenArm arm
  • Provides core SDK and engineering capability
  • Provides production and market operations
Co-built outcomes and rights
  • Jointly develop customized teaching robots
  • Co-create curricula with IP owned by the university
  • Campus commercialization and revenue sharing
University benefits
  • Own a robot brand with independent IP
  • Talent and technology outcomes commercialized together
  • Build model industry-education-research projects
Model 1 · Basic Fast deployment

Industry-education lab co-building

Focused on teaching and training, quickly filling AI/robotics lab gaps to build a model lab.

Integrated hardware + curriculum delivery
  • Provides Mini Pi / Pi+ / Mini Hi robots
  • Standard course packs (basics, motion, ROS)
  • Lab guides, teaching PPTs, case code libraries
Tiered faculty and student training
  • Faculty training: operations, teaching, SDK development
  • Student project training: motion, algorithms, secondary development
Basic competition support
  • Class-A competition resource pack
  • Past winning cases
University benefits
  • Quickly build a standard embodied AI training lab
  • Improve teaching quality and hands-on skills
  • Boost competition strength at low cost
Model 2 · Advanced Research + talent

Joint research institute

Centered on research and talent, co-build an embodied intelligence research institute focused on R&D and commercialization.

Joint R&D platform
  • University provides space and R&D teams
  • WWNT provides hardware, engineering, SDK toolchain
  • Joint provincial/national research grants
Faculty and student deep involvement
  • Product iteration and scenario adaptation
  • Market research and user feedback
  • Top projects enter the product roadmap
Customized teaching and research
  • Custom teaching cases and lab projects
  • Ongoing support, firmware and SDK updates
University benefits
  • Build a high-level research platform for grants
  • Faculty and students deeply involved in real product R&D
  • Accumulate differentiated research outcomes
Model 3 · On-campus Factory Real production line · industry-education

On-campus factory co-building

Bring a real robot production line to campus, driven by real orders, so faculty and students complete assembly, debugging, and testing — integrating teaching, training, and production.

Real production line on campus
  • Standard layout: raw materials, assembly, debugging, testing, packaging
  • Enterprise imports real orders and standard processes
  • University provides space, infrastructure, and production teams
Students in real production
  • Full involvement in assembly, wiring, calibration, and software deployment
  • Full performance testing with standardized acceptance reports
  • On-site enterprise engineers train and assess to real engineering standards
Two-way value loop
  • Enterprise gains stable capacity and standardized delivery
  • University gains real engineering practice and benchmark cases
  • Students gain real project experience and employability
University benefits
  • Build an integrated teaching-training-production platform
  • Deep participation in real production significantly boosts engineering skills
  • Build an industry-education benchmark supporting "Double High" and vocational upgrades
Model 4 · Deep Research-production integration

Outcome commercialization

Centered on research commercialization, jointly advancing faculty/student outcomes through engineering, productization, and marketization to close the loop.

Joint company / project incubation
  • Co-found project companies or incubate projects
  • University invests technology/team/space
  • Clear IP ownership and revenue sharing
Product engineering and deployment
  • Engineering optimization of prototypes
  • Hardware adaptation and stability testing
  • Mass production and market sales
Talent pipeline and employment
  • Order-based talent training
  • Internship and employment matching for top students
  • Industry-oriented course offerings
University benefits
  • Closed-loop commercialization from lab to market
  • Build an industry-education-research benchmark
  • Broaden student employment channels
